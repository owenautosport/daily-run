Privacy

Daily Run website and browser extension · updated 9 November 2026

The short version

Daily Run keeps your game results on your own device. It has no server, no accounts, no analytics and no ads, and it never sends your data anywhere.

What it stores

  • Your results for each daily game (the date, puzzle number, score, and the emoji grid from the game's share text).
  • Your settings: which games are in your run, and whether the run opens automatically each day.

The website keeps these in your browser's local storage. The extension keeps them in chrome.storage.local. Both stay on your device. Uninstalling the extension or clearing site data deletes them. You can export or delete them yourself under Settings.

What the extension reads

  • It runs only on the daily game sites it supports (listed in its manifest), such as nytimes.com/games/wordle and framed.wtf.
  • On those pages it watches for the game's own Share action and reads the share text, e.g. "Wordle 1,969 4/6" and its grid. That's all it reads. It does not read what you type, other pages, your browsing history, or anything else.
  • It does not change how the games work, apart from showing a small Daily Run panel in the corner.

Permissions

  • Storage: to save your results and settings on your device.
  • Idle: to tell when you're back at your computer, so it can open the day's first game once a day. You can turn this off in Settings.
  • Access to the game sites: to show the panel and read each game's share text when you press Share.

Third parties

Daily Run shares nothing with anyone. The games themselves are run by other companies (the New York Times, Teuteuf and so on). When you play them, their own privacy policies apply.
